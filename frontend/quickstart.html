AvTrack Quick-Start

Get from "never opened this" to logging your first comms entry, in about five minutes.

1. Get an account

1There's no self-serve sign-up. Ask whoever's administering AvTrack for your mission to create you a CAP ID + password + authenticator (TOTP) account. You'll get a QR code to scan into an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Authy, 1Password, etc.) at setup time.
2Open AvTrack in a browser and log in with your CAP ID, password, and the current 6-digit code from your authenticator app.

2. Find or create your mission

1In the left panel, under Mission, type into the "Join a mission…" box to search for one you're already a member of (by name or mission number), or use + New mission if this is a brand-new one.
2Once created or joined, select it from the dropdown above "+ New mission."
This is easy to miss: creating or joining a mission doesn't automatically put you "in" it for the session — you have to also pick it from that dropdown. Once selected, a members list and Leave/Close/Reopen controls appear below it, and the rest of the app (aircraft roster, sorties, comms log) scopes itself to that mission.

3. Find your aircraft on the map

1Aircraft already flying with a CAP/PARD-shaped callsign show up on the map automatically — no setup needed. The right-hand panel's Aircraft Aloft tab only shows aircraft that are both in your roster and currently airborne; click Mission Status instead for a per-sortie operational view (assigned area, ETA, overdue flags) of your own mission's sorties.
2To track a tail that isn't flying yet, add it to the roster: in the right panel, upload a CSV (tail_number,callsign,callsign_prefix) with the Add button above the aircraft list, or just wait — if it starts flying with a CAP-shaped callsign, it'll show on the map as a "discovered" (amber, dashed) marker even before you've added it anywhere.
3Click any aircraft (on the map or in the side list) to open its sortie panel at the bottom-left.

4. Log a comms entry

1With the sortie panel open, use the row of quick-select buttons: Engine Start, Wheels Up, In-Grid, Out-Grid, Find, PIREP, Ops Check, RTB, Wheels Down, Engine Stop — the same vocabulary as a real radio call.
2Click one, fill in only the fields that type actually needs (time defaults to now — click "Now" to refresh it), and submit. The panel re-populates itself so you can log another entry of the same type right away (handy for several In-Grid calls in a row).
Logging/editing times requires your mission role to be COMMS, AOBD, IC, or Ops (set when you're added to the mission). Anyone can still view the sortie panel and history. If you're the aircraft's own pilot, you can still correct a logged time on your own sortie even without one of those roles.

5. Wrap up

1When the exercise or mission ends, open the Mission dropdown, select it, and click Close mission. (Reopen mission is there if you close it too early.)
2Need a record of everything logged? Use Comms Log Report in the left panel — pick a UTC start/end and click Generate PDF.

That's the five-minute version. For everything else — airspace overlays, GIS layers, the Ops Board, discovered-aircraft approval, timezones, and more — see the full manual.
